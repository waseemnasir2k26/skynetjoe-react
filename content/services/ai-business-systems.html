What is an AI business systems audit?

An AI business systems audit is an end-to-end review of a company's SaaS stack, ops workflows, data ownership model, and AI surface area, producing a 90-day plan to consolidate redundant tools, retire dead software spend, and AI-enable the workflows that materially move revenue. SkynetLabs delivers the audit, the tool-stack diagram, the build backlog, and the change-management script needed to get a small/mid team to actually adopt the new stack.

What we deliver

An operating blueprint, not a slide deck

Stack and cost audit

Every SaaS tool, monthly cost, seat count, last-login data, owner. A written kill / merge / keep list with refund or annual-saving estimates.

Process map by function

One page per business function (sales, ops, finance, marketing): the as-is process, the bottlenecks, the swivel-chair moments, the data ownership gaps. Drawn, not deck-bulleted.

AI surface area assessment

Which tasks are realistic for AI today (classification, drafting, retrieval). Which are 18-24 months out (judgement-heavy, regulated). Which to never automate (cultural, relational).

90-day execution plan

Three sprints, each with a clear deliverable, owner, success metric, and rollback path. Not "transform the business" — "ship this thing by this date".

Vendor + hiring recommendation

Which work to do internally, which to outsource, which to hire for. Named alternatives for each SaaS tool with annual savings. Honest list — including when to fire your current agency.

Implementation handoff (optional)

If you want, we ship the plan ourselves through our automation, development, and content services. If not, the plan is portable — any agency or in-house team can execute.

McKinsey will charge you $400k for a deck and never write a line of code. Boutique AI consultancies will sell you "AI strategy" without ever opening your tool stack. We do both halves — strategy on Monday, implementation by Friday — because we're a small team that has to ship, not a partner deck protecting a billable hour.

Questions

Common questions

Why work with a small agency over a Big-4 consultancy?
Speed and accountability. A Big-4 engagement starts at six figures, takes nine months, and ends with a deck the team can't operate. We deliver in four weeks, write the report ourselves, and stake our name on shipping the implementation if you want us to. There's nobody to hide behind — which is the point.
What if our leadership disagrees with the audit?
Good — that's the whole point of an outside read. We share the draft before the final and pressure-test our findings against the people who'll have to live with the decisions. We've changed our recommendation in 6 of the last 10 engagements after leadership pushback. The final plan reflects what the team can actually execute, not what we'd love in a vacuum.
Will you tell us to fire people?
Almost never. Most "transform with AI" advice is just "replace your team with software". We've watched that fail too many times. The recommendation is usually role-shifting — promote your best ops person to own the new automation, retrain two contractors into a shared queue, kill a vendor relationship that's eating margin. We name names only when the data is unambiguous.
Can you guarantee a specific ROI?
No, and anyone who does is selling fiction. We commit to specific operational changes — SaaS bill reduction, process step reduction, response-time improvement — and we'll show you the math for projected savings. Whether the savings show up depends on whether the team actually executes the plan after we hand it over.
Who reads the final report inside our company?
CEO, COO, CFO at minimum. We design the deliverable to be readable in 90 minutes by a board, and to be operational by anyone leading a function. We discourage "summary for the team" versions because the kill list is the point — softening it just to keep people comfortable defeats the purpose of the engagement.
