Why do roofing quotes go cold?

A roof is a big, slow decision. Homeowners compare, wait for an insurance answer, or talk it over with a partner. The quote itself is rarely the problem. The silence afterwards is. Most small roofing firms follow up when they remember to, which usually means when the diary is quiet, not when the customer is deciding.

The cost is easy to count. Take your last ten quotes, work out how many got no follow-up at all, and multiply by your average job value. Even one extra closed roof a quarter from quotes that would otherwise have lapsed can pay for the setup. That is arithmetic you can do with your own numbers, not a promise from us.

How does the n8n quote follow-up workflow work?

The workflow is short on purpose. It does one job: stop a live quote from going quiet, and stop the moment a human answers.

StepWhat happensTool
1. Quote goes outWhen you mark a quote as sent in your sheet, CRM or form, n8n receives it and stores the customer name, contact and quote date.Webhook or sheet trigger
2. Wait 2 daysn8n pauses. If the customer has not replied, it sends a short, friendly check-in: did the quote arrive, any questions?Wait + text/email node
3. Wait until day 5Still silent, so a second message goes out, a little warmer, offering to walk through the quote or adjust the scope.Wait + text/email node
4. Final nudge on day 10A last polite message that leaves the door open without pressure.Wait + text/email node
5. Reply pings youAny reply stops the sequence and sends you an alert with the customer's words, so you can pick up the phone yourself.IF node + notification

The important design choice is the stop condition. A follow-up that keeps firing after the customer says yes is worse than no follow-up. Every step checks for a reply first.

What you need to run it

  • An n8n instance (self-hosted or n8n cloud)
  • A place your quotes live: a Google Sheet, your CRM, or a simple form is enough
  • A way to send messages: email is easiest, SMS needs a provider such as Twilio
  • Your own wording for the three messages, in your voice
  • Customers' permission to be contacted by the channel you use

Honest limits

  • It does not write the quote or price the job. It only chases a quote you already sent.
  • Replies by phone call or in person are invisible to the workflow unless you mark the quote as won or lost, so keep the status updated.
  • SMS carries consent rules that vary by country. Email is the safer first channel.
  • Three messages is a ceiling, not a target. If a customer says no, the workflow should stop and stay stopped.

Frequently asked questions

How many follow-ups should a roofer send after a quote?

The video uses three: day two, day five and day ten. That is an example schedule, not a rule. Many owners find two or three polite touches is the point where more messages start to annoy rather than help.

Will the customer know it is automated?

The messages are written in your voice and signed by you, and they are short. If the customer replies, a human (you) takes over. We recommend never pretending the message is something it is not.

Do I need to know how to code to run this?

No. n8n is visual, so each step is a block you can open and edit. Connecting your quote source and your messaging account is the part most owners want help with.

Can this work for other trades?

Yes. Any business that sends quotes and waits, such as builders, electricians, landscapers or cleaners, can reuse the same pattern with different wording and timing.
