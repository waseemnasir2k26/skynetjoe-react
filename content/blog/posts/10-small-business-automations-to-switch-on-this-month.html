Plenty of small businesses lose jobs to whoever answered first, not to a better competitor. The phone rings on a ladder, the message arrives during a showing, the regular goes quiet and nobody notices for weeks.

None of the ten fixes below is clever. Each plugs one specific leak in one specific kind of business, and each lists what you need to have in place. Pick the one that sounds like your week.

HVAC companies: a monthly tune-up reminder

Last year's customers forgot their tune-up, and now it is freezing and they are calling your rival. Your list is full of people who are due for service, and nobody is reminding them.

Each month, the automation finds who is due and writes a friendly text with a booking link. You read it, tap approve, and it sends. When they say yes, the visit lands in your calendar.

Needs: your customer list with last service dates, and a texting number.

Cleaning companies: a rebooking text after every clean

You cleaned their home once, they loved it, and they never booked again. Not because they were unhappy, but because nobody asked.

A few weeks after each clean, the automation sends a short text: want the same day again? One tap on yes and the visit is booked. If they ignore it, it tries once more and then stops, so there is no chasing and no pestering.

Needs: your client list with clean dates, and a texting number. Only text customers who agreed to hear from you; check the texting rules where you operate.

Roofers: a text back when you miss the call

A call missed on a ladder is a roof job that rings the next roofer. The automation texts the caller back right away, asks for the address and the problem, and puts the answer on one list so your lunchtime callback starts informed. The full walkthrough is in missed call text-back for trades, step by step.

Needs: a phone line that can report unanswered calls, and a texting number.

Snow removal companies: a storm notice for the whole route

A storm is coming tonight and dozens of customers are phoning and texting to ask if you are coming. You are plowing, not typing, and some of them never get an answer.

One tap tells every customer on tonight's route that you are coming and to expect you before morning. Anyone who replies "skip" gets marked, so you do not waste a stop.

Needs: your route list with customer numbers, and a texting number. Only text customers who agreed to hear from you; check the texting rules where you operate.

Real estate agents: a first reply while you are mid-showing

A buyer messages several agents about the same house, and only one replies in time. You were at a showing, the reply waited until you got back, and the buyer did not.

A new inquiry lands on your list and the automation drafts a short reply that names the property and offers two viewing times. You approve it from your phone between showings, and the buyer has their options while you are still on site.

Needs: your inquiry inbox and the viewing times you can offer.

Plumbers: a quote from a photo, often within the hour

A photo of a leaking pipe arrives while you are under a sink. The automation drafts a quote from your own price list, plus a short note for the customer, and waits on your phone for you to check it. See the plumbing half of the trades walkthrough.

Needs: your price list, and one place for customers to send photos.

Restaurant managers: shift cover in one tap

It is Friday evening and a cook calls in sick. Now you are texting the whole team one by one, half of them do not answer, and the ones who do reply to the wrong thread.

One tap sends the open shift to everyone who can cover it. The first person to say yes gets the shift, everyone else is told it is filled, and the rota updates itself. There is a longer write-up in our restaurant shift cover post.

Needs: your team's contact list and your rota.

Distributors: a nudge when a regular goes quiet

Your best customers reorder every few weeks. This month one of them went quiet, and you noticed too late. They did not leave, they just ordered from whoever messaged first.

The automation watches each customer's usual rhythm. When someone is overdue, it drafts a WhatsApp message: shall we send your usual order? You approve it, they reply yes, and the order drops into your sheet.

Needs: each customer's order history and a WhatsApp number.

Restaurant owners: draft replies to every Google review

Weeks of reviews and no replies, and every new customer reads that silence. You mean to answer, but service never stops, and writing nice replies late at night is nobody's idea of fun.

Each new review gets a draft reply in your own tone, thankful for the good ones and calm for the bad ones. Nothing is published until you have read it. See also Google review reply automation.

Needs: access to your Google Business Profile reviews.

Garages: winter tire reminders before the first frost

At the first frost your phone explodes. Everyone needs winter tires this week and you cannot fit them all, because they all wait until it freezes and nobody asked them earlier.

Before the cold arrives, the automation texts each customer a time to swap, spread across the quiet weeks. They tap a slot and it lands in your calendar, so there is no panic week.

Needs: your customer list with numbers, and your open slots. Only text customers who agreed to hear from you; check the texting rules where you operate.
