Why do follow-ups slip?

Follow-up is rarely skipped on purpose. It slips because it is boring, easy to forget and slightly uncomfortable. You are busy delivering the work you already won, so chasing the next deal sits at the bottom of the list.

The result is predictable. Prospects who were interested simply moved on to whoever replied, and you never find out. Many owners can name a handful of deals they believe went quiet because they did not chase them.

Reminders and a spreadsheet help, but they still rely on you to write each message and remember to send it. The weak link is still a busy person.

How the automation works, step by step

  1. Start from an event. The automation begins when something happens, such as a proposal being sent or a form being submitted. The prospect is added to a follow-up sequence.
  2. Choose a sensible rhythm. You set the gaps, for example a short check-in after two days, a useful note after a week, and a polite final nudge later. Spacing matters more than volume.
  3. Personalise each message. An AI model uses what you know about the prospect, such as their name, what you proposed and what they asked about, so the message reads as a considered note, not a mass mailing.
  4. Watch for a reply. The moment the prospect responds, the sequence stops. Nobody should receive a "just checking in" email after they have already answered.
  5. Hand over at the right moment. When a prospect says yes, asks a question or wants to talk, the automation offers a booking link or alerts you so you can take over personally.

What you need to get started

  • A list of the follow-up points you would send if you had unlimited time.
  • Access to your email and, if you use one, your CRM or deal list.
  • A short description of your tone, plus two or three example messages you like.
  • A clear rule for when the automation stops and you step in.

Honest limits

Automated follow-up should support relationships, not replace them. High-value deals usually deserve a personal call or message from you, and the automation is best used to make sure that call actually happens.

Over-sending is a real risk. Limit the number of messages, always stop on reply, and honour unsubscribe requests. Depending on where your prospects are, rules on commercial email may apply, so check what is required for your market.

The AI can only personalise with the information it has. If your records are thin, the messages will be generic.

Frequently asked questions

Will follow-up emails sound robotic?

They do not have to. When the automation uses real details about the prospect and your own tone of voice, messages read like short personal notes. Review the first batch before sending to check.

How do I make sure it stops when someone replies?

The automation checks for replies before each send and stops the sequence immediately when it finds one. This is one of the first things we test.

How many follow-ups should I send?

There is no universal number. Many people send three to five spaced touches. Start with a modest sequence and adjust based on replies and any complaints.

Does it work for proposals, quotes and enquiries?

Yes. Any moment where a prospect has something from you and has not answered can start a sequence.
