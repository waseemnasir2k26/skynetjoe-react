In June 2026 Google gave site owners two new things inside Search Console: a report on how your pages appear in its generative AI features, and a control that lets a site opt out of those features. Both reached every website worldwide by 31 August 2026. If you run a local or service business, you probably have a Search Console account already, so this affects you. Here is what each piece does, what Google says and does not say, and a short monthly routine. Every date below comes from Google's own pages, which I read on 10 November 2026.

What launched, and when

Google announced the control and the reporting on 3 June 2026. Its Search Central blog post that day introduced the "Search Generative AI performance reports", and a post on Google's own blog by Mrinalini Loew, General Manager of the Search Ecosystem, described a control Google was "beginning to test" for how links and content appear in generative AI features.

The rollout language is worth a careful read. The original text says Google was starting with a subset of site owners (the Google blog names the UK), then going wider. Both posts now carry a note dated 31 August 2026 saying the features have "rolled out to all websites worldwide". Search Engine Roundtable reported the same on 1 September 2026 and added that if you cannot see the report, your site may not have enough data yet.

What the Search Console report shows

The report covers AI Overviews and AI Mode in Search, plus generative AI features in Discover. From Google's announcement, it gives you:

  • Impressions: how often URLs from your site appeared in these features.
  • Pages: which URLs appeared.
  • Countries: where people saw you.
  • Devices: available for Search results only.
  • Dates: hourly, daily, weekly and monthly views.

What it does not list: clicks, position or click-through rate. Search Engine Roundtable's write-up says the same, adding that there is no query data either. Google says it wants to learn which insights are most useful and may add metrics over time. So for now this is a visibility report. It tells you which pages are being shown, not whether those showings brought in customers. For that you still need the normal Performance report and your own enquiry tracking.

Google's AI features documentation also says traffic from these features is part of your overall search traffic and shows up in the Performance report under the "Web" search type. There is no separate click number to hunt for.

One practical way to use it: pick your three most valuable pages, such as a main service page, a pricing page and a location page, and see whether they appear at all. A page that never appears is not necessarily broken, but it is the first place to look for thin content, unclear wording or an indexing problem. A page that appears often but earns few enquiries is a different problem, usually the offer or the next step on the page.

What the opt-out control does

It is a single site-wide switch in Search Console. Google's control-what-you- share documentation describes it as managing "your site's inclusion in generative AI features on Google Search, including AI Overviews and AI Mode". The announcement adds AI Overviews in Discover and puts the consequence bluntly: opted-out sites "will not receive traffic or impressions from our generative AI features". It also says the control "will not be used as a ranking signal" for results outside those features.

What the announcement does not cover:

  • AI training. The post does not say whether the switch touches training or model use. Google's AI features documentation points to the Google-Extended robots.txt token for AI training and grounding in other Google systems. Treat the two as separate controls.
  • Timing. Nothing I read says how quickly a change applies.
  • Pages. The control is site-wide. For one page, Google's documentation lists nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex as the ways to limit what Search shows.

Google updated its control-what-you-share documentation on 21 October 2026 to list the new control alongside the older ones.

Should a service business opt out?

Almost certainly not, and this is my opinion rather than Google's. If you want enquiries from search, appearing in AI answers is one more place a customer can meet your name. Opting out means you stop appearing there, with no stated upside for your normal rankings. Google says it will not use the setting as a ranking signal outside AI features, which means the switch is a visibility trade, not an SEO lever.

Cases where you might think about it: you publish content you are paid to keep behind a wall, you have legal or contractual limits on how your text is reused, or a page that must be exact (such as a dosage chart) should never appear as a summary. In those cases, start with page-level controls and only go site-wide if the whole site has the same problem. Whatever you choose, write down the date and the reason so you can read the later numbers properly.

What Google says helps you appear

Google published an AI optimization guide on 15 May 2026, and added a clarification on llms.txt on 15 June 2026. Its position is plainer than most of what you read online:

  • To appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Search with a snippet. Google says there are no additional technical requirements.
  • You do not need new machine-readable files, AI text files or markup. On llms.txt, the guide says you can create one for other services, but "Google Search ignores them".
  • There is no special schema.org markup needed for generative AI search. Structured data is still worth having for rich results.
  • You do not need to break content into tiny pieces for AI.
  • Focus on content that is helpful, reliable and written for people, with first-hand insight.

A 30-minute monthly check

  1. Open the report (5 min). In Search Console, find the generative AI report under Performance. If it is missing, note the date and check again next month.
  2. Export the page list (5 min). Which URLs are shown? Are they the pages you want customers to meet first?
  3. Check countries (2 min). Any surprises for a local business?
  4. Compare with the Performance report (7 min). Did clicks to those same pages move? Remember the AI report holds impressions only.
  5. Look at your enquiries (5 min). Ask new customers how they found you. This answers what no report can.
  6. Fix one thing (5 min). Usually a thin page: add the detail a real customer would ask for.
  7. Write down the control's state (1 min). Opted in or out, with the date.
Short version: read the report, leave the switch alone unless you have a specific reason, and spend the time on pages worth being shown.

Questions people ask

When did the Search Console AI report and opt-out control launch?

Google announced both on 3 June 2026 and started with a subset of sites. A note dated 31 August 2026 says they have rolled out to all websites worldwide.

Does the Search Console report show clicks from AI Overviews?

No. The report shows impressions, pages, countries, devices for Search and dates. Clicks from these features sit inside your overall Performance report under the Web search type.

Does opting out of AI Overviews hurt my normal rankings?

Google says the control will not be used as a ranking signal for results outside its generative AI features. Opting out does remove your traffic and impressions from those features.

Do I need llms.txt to appear in Google AI Overviews?

No. Google's AI optimization guide says you can keep llms.txt files for other services, but Google Search ignores them.
